# Film and video production ledger

Each row names a production technique, describes what a creator sees or hears, and gives a repeatable check. A **native control** has an authored N4 representation. A **library effect** packages a reusable treatment. A **project effect** is supplied by the production. **Open** means this ledger has no public demonstration of the full check. A narrower control can still be linked as evidence. [Regression guide](../../../../docs/tests.md) · [Production recipes](production-recipes.md)

## Animation and motion graphics

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Animate on twos** (shoot on twos, animation on twos) | Expose each new drawing or pose for two consecutive output frames. At 24 fps, a character can take 12 poses per second while the background moves on every frame. | Play a 24-frame shot with the character on twos and a background pan on ones. Step through all 24 frames: only the character repeats in pairs. Seek straight to the second frame of a pair and compare it with export. | Open. |
| **Kinetic typography** | Use the timing, movement, or changing form of type as part of the performance. | Change a word and delay its entrance by six frames. Check that the rest of the headline keeps its timing. | Native text-range selector with fill and offset. [Text evaluation](../../../engine/src/graphics/text_animation.rs) |
| **Line boil**, **texture boil** | A held drawing stays alive through small changes to its contours. Variation in the pencil or ink texture can continue on a separate rhythm. | Change the word in a held title. Freeze the contour poses while varying the ink, then hold the ink constant while the contours move. Compare a direct seek with the same frame in export. | Open. |
| **Gate weave** | Registration varies as film passes through a gate. All marks printed on the same film frame share the resulting movement. | Put a registration cross beside a title on the same plate. Both should move together relative to the screen edge. Give one word an independent boil and verify the two movements can be adjusted separately. | Open. |
| **Draw-on** (write-on, for lettering) | Reveal a line as if someone were drawing it along its course. | Draw a route that doubles back. Pause halfway and check that the tip has followed the bend. Shorten the route and replay the reveal. | Native trim on an authored stroke. [Signal Atlas route](../../../examples/signal-atlas/sequences/survey.xml) · [Trim semantics](trim-paths.md) |
| **Lower third** | A graphic in the lower part of the picture, often identifying a speaker or supplying context. | Put a lower third over moving footage. Scrub its entrance and check that the picture behind it keeps its own timing. | A title is built from position, text and animation. The revision exercise is in the [creator brief](creator-expectations.md). |

## Camera, lenses, and finishing

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Color grade** | Shape the tone or color of a shot. A scanned print can have warmer paper and denser ink while the route above it keeps its own color. | Adjust blue gain on the survey plate. Check the route, the second use of the plate, and a partially transparent edge. | Native per-channel gain and offset. [Source effects](source-effects.md) · [Grade checks](../../../engine/tests/rgb_grade.rs) |
| **Motion blur** (shutter angle) | Movement during the exposure spreads an edge along its direction of travel. A longer exposure at the same frame rate generally leaves a longer trail. | Move a white bar past a stationary mark. Compare short and long shutter angles at the same frame. The bar’s trail should grow while the mark stays sharp. Step across a hard cut and check the incoming picture. | Open. |
| **Focus pull** (rack focus) | The point of attention moves from one subject in focus to another. An editor can simulate this on a flat graphic by moving a band of sharp detail. | Park between two street names and move the sharp band onto the engraving between them. The overlaid marker should keep its defined edge. | Native feathered source blur on a 2D plate. Optical focus and depth of field need a separate trial. [Source blur checks](../../../engine/tests/source_blur.rs) |
| **Chromatic aberration** | A lens can bring colors to different positions or depths of focus, producing colored fringes on high-contrast detail. | For lateral aberration, frame white marks at the optical center and near the corners. Increase the effect and inspect the stronger color separation toward the edges. At zero strength the marks should be neutral again. | Open. |
| **Bloom** | Bright highlights spill a soft glow into the surrounding picture. | Move a small bright light beside a dim gray card on black. Raise the bloom and look for a halo extending beyond the light while distant dark detail stays crisp. | Open. |
| **Push-in** | The framing moves closer to a subject. A product edit can stage a digital push-in on part of a screen recording. | Replace the take so the button moves. Check that the push-in lands on the new button and the attached callout remains readable. | Native camera framing and zoom. Subject tracking and callout layout need a separate trial. [Camera checks](../../../engine/tests/camera.rs) |

## Titles over footage, mattes and graphic finishes

Keep foreground and backdrop independently editable. Refraction bends a line behind a transparent edge. **Coalescence** joins the rims of two approaching shapes and separates them again as they move apart. To qualify either look, change the footage, move a sharp line above and below the shapes, and check the same frame after a direct seek. These are candidate project or library effects. [Backdrop input contract](rendering-pipeline.md#shader-contract)

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Track matte** (alpha matte, luma matte) | Use a layer’s opacity or brightness to reveal another layer. An editable word can show footage through the letters. | Fill “open” with moving footage. The hole in the “o” stays clear. Change the copy, then try a fully opaque black-to-white ramp as the matte. The ramp gives a graded reveal only in luma mode. | Native shape masks cover the alpha case for supported geometry. Luma mattes and arbitrary text/footage matte sources need a separate trial. [Mask checks](../../../engine/tests/path.rs) |
| **Multiply** | A highlighter darkens the colors underneath. White ink leaves the picture alone, and dark engraved detail shows through colored ink. | Hold a mark over changing footage, then move it onto another street. Both the mark’s softened edge and the lettering under it should follow the new overlap. | Native multiply blend and group isolation. [Blend checks](../../../engine/tests/multiply.rs) |
| **Screen** and **Add** (Linear Dodge) | Two ways to mix a bright foreground with the plate underneath. Add sums the light contributions. Screen approaches white more gradually in the usual normalized range. | Blend an opaque 50% gray title across a 50% gray patch in the same working space. Before output conversion, Screen gives 0.75 and Add gives 1.0. Check a half-covered letter edge as well. | Open for both modes. Multiply has its own entry. |
| **Light wrap** | The background casts some of its color just inside a foreground silhouette, helping the two feel photographed together. | Pass a red light, then a blue light, behind one edge of a gray letter. Only the nearby inner rim should pick up that color. A fixed glow will miss the change. | Open. The masked backdrop is available as a compositor input. [Backdrop checks](../../../engine/tests/backdrop.rs) |
| **Grain dissolve** (noise-driven matte) | Reveal or erode the picture in small flecks as a threshold travels through a noise pattern. | Animate a word from solid to broken to clear. Freeze halfway and replace the plate. With a fixed noise matte, the holes stay in the same places and reveal the new picture. Seek back to that frame. | Open. |
| **Displacement map** (turbulent displace for a noise-driven warp) | A control image shifts where the picture is sampled. A letter edge can ripple or pull sideways. | Warp a straight stem and the hole in an “o”. A neutral map should restore both. Animate the map and check that the contours bend while a registration mark on the plate stays still. | Open. |
| **Bevel and emboss**, **specular highlight**, **chrome type** | A bevel gives an edge apparent relief. Narrow highlights and dark or colored reflection bands can suggest polished metal. | Rotate the apparent light on “shade”. The highlight should move around both the outer edge and the hole in the “a”. Edit the copy and increase output scale, then inspect rim width and corners. | Open. |
| **Film grain**, **grain matching** | Add fine variation across an image or match a new graphic’s texture to photographed material. | Lay clean gray type over a grainy plate. Match the apparent grain size and strength at the edge. Advance one frame, then return. An animated grain pattern should change on advance and repeat on return. | Open. |
| **Diffusion**, **halation** | Diffusion softens detail and spreads highlights. Film halation can add a warm fringe where strong exposure spills across a light-to-dark boundary. | Put white lettering and a dim gray patch on black. Adjust the broad glow, then the warm rim independently. The dark field away from the highlights should keep its original color. | Open. |
| **Masked adjustment layer** | Apply one finish to the combined picture below a layer, within a chosen area. Titles placed above the adjustment can stay clean. | Put a halftone window above moving footage and below a caption. Move the caption below the adjustment. Only the part of the caption inside the window should now break into dots. | Native masked blur over the scoped backdrop. Arbitrary adjustment stacks need a separate trial. [Backdrop checks](../../../engine/tests/backdrop.rs) |
| **Halftone screen** | Represent tone with a pattern of ink dots. In a conventional amplitude-modulated screen, darker areas get larger dots at a given screen pitch and angle. | Run a grayscale ramp and a moving hand through a rectangular window. Check dot size across the ramp, rotate the screen, and verify that the caption above the window stays clean. | Open. |
| **Ordered dither** | Use a repeating threshold pattern to represent intermediate tones with a limited palette. The matrix and its alignment affect the visible texture. | Reduce the same ramp to black and white using a fixed matrix. Pause on middle gray, slide the footage under the window, and check whether the pattern stays registered to the intended grid. | Open. |

## Picture and sound editing

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hard cut** (straight cut) | One shot replaces another between adjacent frames. | Step from frame 63 to 64 in Signal Atlas. The opening map gives way to the sounding shot on the cut. | Native placement boundary. [Signal Atlas master](../../../examples/signal-atlas/project.proj) |
| **J-cut** (split edit) | The incoming shot’s sound starts while the outgoing picture is still on screen. | Listen for the incoming voice before the five-second picture cut, then step the picture across the join. | Audio and picture placements have independent timing. [Master contract](../../../../docs/project-format.md) |
| **Slip edit** | Change which part of a source plays inside a clip while keeping its timeline position and duration. | Slip the presenter picture by 24 frames. Its entrance, exit, underlying screen recording, and narration should keep their timing. | Open. |
| **Handles** (head and tail handles) | Extra source footage before the in-point or after the out-point, available when extending a shot. | Ask for twelve more frames of a VFX shot. On its return, step across both joins and check for missing or repeated pictures. | Open. |

## Sound editing, mixing, and delivery

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **LPF** (low-pass filter, high cut) | Attenuate frequencies above a chosen cutoff. A mixer might lower the cutoff on music as a door closes to make it sound muffled. | Mix equal-level 250 Hz and 8 kHz tones. Set the cutoff to 1 kHz and the slope to 12 dB/octave. The low tone should stay near its original level and the high tone should fall substantially. Compare bypass, preview, and export. | Open. |
| **HPF** (high-pass filter, low cut) | Attenuate frequencies below a chosen cutoff, commonly to reduce rumble in dialogue. | Add 40 Hz rumble to a voice take. With a 100 Hz low cut, listen for less rumble and check that a 1 kHz reference tone stays close to its original level. | Open. |
| **Parametric EQ** (equalization) | Shape the tone of a recording. For a bell band the mixer selects a center frequency, gain, and bandwidth or Q. | Cut a boxy resonance in a voice take. Narrow the band at the same center frequency and gain, then check how much of the neighboring frequencies recover. | Open. |
| **De-hum** | Remove a steady electrical hum, often at 50 or 60 Hz and its harmonics. A narrow notch filter can target an individual tone. | Use a fresh take with 60 Hz and 180 Hz hum throughout. Switch the treatment on halfway. Listen to the dialogue and measure both tones before and after the switch. | Open. |
| **Clip gain** | Raise or lower the level of one audio clip. | Set one clip to half amplitude (about −6 dB). At an overlap, verify its contribution is halved while the other clip keeps its level. | Native fixed gain. [Sample-level mix checks](../../../engine/tests/export_audio.rs) |
| **Room tone** | Record the quiet sound of a location and use it to preserve continuity across dialogue edits. | Shorten an interview answer and fill the pause from the same room. Listen across the join with eyes closed for a sudden drop to silence or an audible loop. | Open. |
| **Audio crossfade** | Fade one sound out while another fades in over the same interval. | Join two takes of steady room ambience with a 100 ms crossfade. Audition for a click or conspicuous level dip, then shorten the overlap and check where the handoff lands. | Open. |
| **Dynamic range compression** | Reduce the level difference between loud and soft passages. Threshold, ratio, attack, and release shape how the compressor responds. | Play alternating quiet and loud phrases with makeup gain off. Listen for reduced peaks on the loud phrases and recovery after they end. | Open. |
| **Ducking** | Lower one sound while another needs attention, typically music under dialogue. A sidechain can use the voice to drive the reduction. | Move a spoken line two seconds later and repeat the ducking pass. Listen for the music to dip under the new entrance and return after the line. | Open. |
| **Stems** | Separate, time-aligned submixes, commonly dialogue, music, and effects, supplied so a mixer can rebalance or replace one part. | Mute the voice stem and check the cue stem against picture. For stem delivery, reimport the separately exported parts at their common start and compare their sum with the approved mix. | Open. |
| **Integrated loudness** (LUFS) | Measure the programme’s perceived loudness over its running time for a delivery specification. | Play the whole programme through a reset meter. Independently meter the exported audio over the same range and compare readings against the brief’s target. | Open. |
| **True peak** (dBTP) | Estimate the highest level of the reconstructed audio signal, including peaks between samples. | Use a tone with a known inter-sample peak. Meter the delivered audio and compare its maximum dBTP with an independent true-peak meter and the brief’s ceiling. | Open. |

## Compositing and graphic finishes

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Picture-in-picture** (PiP, inset) | A second moving picture occupies part of the main picture. | Step through the presenter’s entrance and exit. Compare both visible sources with their originals at the same instant. | Open. |
| **Knockout** | Cut away part of the foreground artwork so the picture behind it shows through. | Put a woven mark over two colors. Both background colors should show through the gaps at the crossings. | Native destination-out coverage. [Knockout checks](../../../engine/src/render/gpu/knockout_tests.rs) |
| **Drop shadow** | An offset shadow follows a foreground element’s silhouette and separates it from the background. | Step the masked presenter out of frame. Its shadow should disappear on the same frame. | Native silhouette-based shadow. [Shadow checks](../../../engine/src/shadow_tests.rs) |
| **Frosted glass** (backdrop blur) | A translucent-looking panel softens the picture passing behind it while foreground lettering stays sharp. | Move a line behind the panel, then in front of it. Inspect the line and the type in both versions. | Native masked backdrop blur. [Backdrop checks](../../../engine/tests/backdrop.rs) |
| **Dry-brush texture** (broken ink) | Leave irregular breaks in a painted mark so the surface underneath shows through. | Roughen the chart axes. Inspect the ticks against the paper and check that the plotted line and titles keep their finish. | Open. |

## Analog video, VHS playback and CRT display

A tape dropout, a composite signal artifact and a display artifact happen at different stages. A title added after digitization can stay sharp over a damaged tape signal and still inherit the final screen treatment.

| Term | Meaning on a production | Picture or sound check | Delivery route and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CRT scanlines and phosphor mask** | A raster CRT can show gaps between illuminated scanlines. A color tube’s phosphor pattern adds a finer dot or stripe texture. | Move a close-up behind the screen. Check that bright strokes broaden, the row centers stay put, and a uniform gray signal reveals three distinct RGB positions in each repeating cell. | Open. |
| **Phosphor persistence** (CRT afterglow) | The light emitted at a screen position decays after excitation. A bright moving mark can leave a fading trace where it was. | Flash a white title for one frame, then show black. Mute the flash in a second take. Seek directly to the same dark frame in both takes and check the trace after the next cut. | The compositor has a bounded history path. A production-quality display response needs its own check. [History pass](../../../engine/src/render/gpu/history.rs) |
| **Chroma bleed and Y/C delay** | Limited color detail can smear a saturated edge sideways. A delay between luma and chroma shifts the colored edge relative to its brightness edge. | Put a red block beside fine white type. Widen the color edge, then shift it right with its width held constant. Inspect the brightness edge and the type in both versions. | Open. |
| **Dot crawl** (chroma crawl) | A composite decoder can interpret some color as brightness. Small dots appear to travel along a saturated boundary. | Hold a red bar against gray. Step through successive frames and watch the dots change phase along the edge. Seek back to the same frame and recover its phase. | Open. |
| **Time-base error** (flagging, horizontal tearing) | Unstable playback timing shifts parts of individual lines sideways. The top of a straight vertical edge can curl or wave. | Bend the top of a stationary grid for six frames. The lower part stays registered and the top straightens again at the authored cue. | Open. |
| **Tracking error** (mistracking) | When a playback head loses alignment with a recorded track, bands of noise can interrupt the picture. | Move a band across lettering recorded with the shot. Add a new caption after the tape treatment and check that it stays readable as the band passes behind it. | Open. |
| **Tape dropout** | A brief loss of recorded signal can appear as a short black or white horizontal streak. | Introduce two brief streaks on different lines. Jump straight to each affected frame, then reverse through it. Their positions and durations should match the approved playback and export. | Open. |
| **Head-switching noise** | The handoff between a VCR’s rotating heads can expose a band of disturbance near the bottom of a full-frame VHS transfer. A television’s overscan can hide it. | Show the full captured frame, then crop the bottom for an overscan view. The disturbance should disappear with the cropped source lines and reappear when the crop is removed. | Open. |
